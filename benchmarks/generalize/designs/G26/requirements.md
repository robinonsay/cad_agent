# G26 Snap tube clip for 12 mm rod: requirements (L1 / L2 / L3)

Training design of the generalization benchmark (`benchmarks/generalize/`). It uses the same grading
scheme as the enclosure control loop (E1-01, `benchmarks/enclosure/requirements.md`): L1 says what
the owner needs, L2 turns it into an engineering requirement with numbers, L3 is a concrete check
that Python runs on the delivered STL. The machine-readable request (L1 only, `dev.json` format) is
`design.json` next to this file. There is one printed body (the clip); the tube and the two screws
are reference models.

**Brief (verbatim, `plan.json`):** "A C-shaped snap clip for a 12 mm tube that mounts to a wall
with two M3 countersunk screws 30 mm apart. The mouth opening is 10.5 mm so the tube pushes in and
snaps; arm thickness 2.2 mm. The C should print with the open side up and its layer lines along the
flex direction."

**How to read this.** Every row has a method (inspection, analysis, demonstration, test, human), a
pass/fail criterion with a number and tolerance, and a weight (3 = critical, 2 = major, 1 = minor).
The Gate column marks the must-pass checks (section 5). Every number that comes from an assumption
(section 3) is provisional. Do not relax a criterion because a design fails it.

## 1. Frames, tolerances and the two interference checks

### 1.1 Frames

- **Clip frame (all coordinates below):** origin on the tube's axis at the clip's mid-length, **Y
  along the tube's axis**, **Z in the mouth direction (up)**, X across the clip, so the lips open
  along X. The wall face (the flange's underside) is the plane Z = −Zw, where Zw is declared in
  `layout.json` (default 9.1: bore radius 6.1 plus 3.0 of base). The screws go in along −Z from the
  top. A design delivered in another orientation is normalised into this frame by rotations of
  multiples of 90° and mirroring before grading (rubric step 1); that is never penalised. The grader
  finds the bore axis by fitting a cylinder to the bore surface and checks it against the declared
  tube axis.
- **Section:** many requirements use the clip's cross-section in the XZ plane (taken at three
  positions along Y: 10 %, 50 %, 90 % of the clip's width, away from holes) and polar angles θ about
  the bore axis, measured from +X towards +Z (the left lip is at θ ≈ 149°, the right lip at 31°).
  Rays are cast from the bore axis.

### 1.2 Measurement tolerances

| Quantity | Tolerance ε | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| length, distance, clearance | 0.02 mm | a value passes if it meets the limit to within ε |
| angle | 0.5° | same |
| interference volume | 0.01 mm³ | "no interference" means intersection volume ≤ 0.01 mm³ |
| hole / bore diameter | largest inscribed circle (bore: the fitted cylinder's diameter) | |
| thickness | median of ray casts over the stated polar range | |

### 1.3 The two interference checks, and designed contact

- **`clear_of(bodies, volume, clearance)`**: the minimum distance between the printed body and
  `volume` is ≥ `clearance` (pass: d ≥ clearance − ε; penetration reported separately).
- **`inside(volume, region, clearance)`**: `volume` lies entirely in `region`, at least `clearance`
  from its boundary.
- The grader builds every reference model and envelope itself from the catalogue and the declared
  layout; the design's own copies are never trusted for grading.

**Designed contact for G26 is not interference.** These pairs are excluded from every interference
and clearance check; only penetration above 0.01 mm³ counts, and a distance of zero passes:
1. the tube resting against the bore wall (the bore is 0.05–0.15 per side larger than the tube, so contact is a rest, not a squeeze);
2. the tube against the two lips while it is being pushed in (L2.3.3): this is the snap; the penetration depth is the lip deflection and is limited by L2.3.3, L2.4.2;
3. a countersunk screw head in its countersink and its shank in its hole;
4. the clip's wall face against the wall plane.

Not designed contact: the tube touching the clip anywhere except the bore wall and the lips; a screw
head standing proud of the surface.

## 3. Assumptions and open questions

Derived from the brief and engineering convention; none needs the owner. Uncertainty is stated so a
grader knows how far to trust a number.

| # | Question | Value chosen | Uncertainty | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | The tube? | A solid rod, OD 12.0, length 40 stand-in (a hollow pipe of the same OD behaves the same). | OD ±0.1 | The brief says "12 mm tube". |
| A2 | Clip width along the tube? | 12.0–30.0 (default 20.0). | | The brief gives none; a clip needs about the tube's diameter or more to resist tilting, and 30 is the most the 50 mm size limit allows comfortably. |
| A3 | Material and strain limit? | PETG, 3.0 % allowable bending strain at the arm root (yield about 4 %); worst-case tolerances 3.5 %. | ±1 % | The arms must open 0.75 per side. PLA (about 1.5 %) would need arms 12.8 long and is not graded. Across-layer strength matters here (see D4): the limit is a conservative PETG value. |
| A4 | Countersunk screw? | ISO 7046-1 M3 flat countersunk head: Ø6.0 top, 90°, about 1.65 high (modelled 1.5 deep to the shank); clearance hole Ø3.4 from `bolt_m3_iso4762`. | ±0.3 on the head diameter | The brief names M3 countersunk screws; the catalogue holds the socket-head M3 for the hole sizes only. |
| A5 | FDM hole allowances? | Hole Ø3.3–3.6; bore Ø12.1–12.3 (tube 12.0 plus 0.05–0.15 per side). | ±0.1 | Printed holes shrink by 0.1–0.2. |
| A6 | Snap formula? | The arm is treated as a straight cantilever of uniform thickness t and free length ℓ, tip deflection δ: strain ε = 3·t·δ / (2·ℓ²). δ = (tube OD − mouth) / 2. ℓ is the arc length of the arm's mid-surface from the lip's inner corner to the first angle where the radial thickness exceeds 1.5 t. | ±30 % | Standard snap-fit beam formula; a curved arm is a little stiffer, so this is slightly conservative. |
| A7 | How much does the tube deflect the arms? | 0.75 per side (12.0 − 10.5 = 1.5 in total, split equally) at the instant the tube's centre passes the lip height. | | Geometry of the brief's numbers. |
| A8 | Which direction are the screws 30 apart? | Across the clip (X), perpendicular to the tube's axis, so the flanges carry the screws. | | A C-clip is narrower along the tube than 30 mm in practice, and the tube sits between the screws. |
| A9 | Wall and fixing? | A flat wall, wood or plastic; the screws are 10–16 long (declared length is not graded). | | Not specified. |
| A10 | Printing? | FDM, PETG, 0.4 nozzle, 0.2 layers, 200 × 200 × 200 bed. | | Common printers. |
| A11 | Size limit? | 50 × 30 × 20 (X × Y × Z). | | A tight design is about 40 × 20 × 13.3 (section 6). |

### 3.1 Fixed decisions (chosen once; no alternatives are graded)

| # | Decision |
|---|---|
| D1 | The grading frame is the clip frame (section 1.1): tube axis Y, mouth +Z, screws across the clip in X. |
| D2 | Exactly two screws, 30.0 apart in X, symmetric about the bore's vertical plane (±0.2), in flanges either side of the C. |
| D3 | The clip is one printed body: a ring with a mouth, a base that closes the bore's underside, and a flange either side. |
| D4 | Print orientation: mouth up (+Z), the flange underside on the bed. The lips open along X, which lies in the layer plane: that is what "layer lines along the flex direction" is measured as (L2.7.1). The weaker across-layer direction is covered by the conservative strain limit (A3). |
| D5 | Countersunk head model: top Ø6.0, 90° cone down to the Ø3.0 shank (1.5 deep). The countersink in the clip is 90° ± 2°, top Ø6.2–6.5, so the head top sits 0.0–0.5 below the flange top. |
| D6 | The mouth opening is the shortest distance between the two lips' inner corners in the section (10.5 ± 0.1). The tube is a rigid reference and the clip is rigid in the checks; deflection is computed with A6, not simulated. |

## 6. Feasibility

A concrete design exists (profile rebuilt and measured in the audit with shapely: no overhang faces
beyond 45°, bed contact 97.7 %). Clip frame, mm.

- **Bore:** Ø12.2 (R 6.1) on the axis at the origin, width 20.0 (Y −10 to 10). The mouth removes the wedge within ±59.39° of +Z, so the inner lip corners are at (±5.25, +3.10): mouth = 10.50. Wrap angle 360° − 2 × 59.39° = 241.2° (200°–260°). Lip end faces are radial planes, 30.6° from horizontal; the outer lip corners are at (±7.13, +4.22), 14.26 apart (≥ 12.5).
- **Arms:** outer radius 8.3 (R + 2.2), thickness 2.2. Below each arm a block of half-width 6.4 (Z down to the flange, up to Z −5.0) closes the ring: the radial thickness exceeds 3.3 (= 1.5 t) first at θ = 223.6° (left), where the ray meets the corner of the block and the flange top at (−6.4, −6.1), so the free arm runs from θ = 223.6° to the lip at 149.4°, ℓ = 74.2° × π/180 × 7.2 = 9.33 (≥ 9.1). δ = 0.75: ε = 3 × 2.2 × 0.75 / (2 × 9.33²) = 2.85 % (≤ 3.0 %); worst case δ = 0.85: 3.23 % (≤ 3.5 %). The outer surface below the equator is the circle down to θ = 219.5° (normal about 40° below horizontal, inside the 45° limit) and the block's vertical wall below it: no overhang over 45°.
- **Base and flange:** flange X −20 to 20, Y −10 to 10, Z −9.1 to −6.1 (3.0 thick); bore bottom at Z −6.1, so 3.0 of base under the tube. Wall face at Z −9.1 (Zw = 9.1).
- **Screws:** holes Ø3.4 at (±15.0, 0): spacing 30.0. Countersink 90°, top Ø6.2, 1.4 deep, so 1.6 remains below (≥ 1.5). Head Ø6.0 sits 0.1 below the flange top (0.0–0.5). Ligament from the countersink edge (x = 18.1) to the flange end 1.9 (≥ 1.5); to the Y edges 6.9; to the arms at least 3.6 (countersink edge 11.9 versus the arm at 8.3).
- **Insertion:** with the tube centre at the lip height Z 3.10, each lip overlaps the tube by 0.75 (0.65–0.85); below that the tube clears the Ø12.2 bore by 0.1 per side.

## 8. Grading rubric (design-specific)

`benchmarks/generalize/rubric.md` did not exist when this design was written, so this section holds
the rules a judge needs for G26. When a generic rubric exists, it applies on top of this section
and, where they differ, this document's numbers win (precedence below). A judge (a capable model,
never the local model under test) gets one design package and decides **MEETS** or **DOES NOT MEET**
against section 4. When it does not, the judge says **where**, precisely enough that a separate agent
can find the gap and make a delta design without seeing the judge's reasoning.

### 8.1 Inputs

| Input | Where | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `clip.stl` | the design package (clip frame, mm) | **every measurement** |
| `.scad` source | package | naming features; finding where a defect comes from; never for measuring |
| renders | package `views/*.png` | locating and illustrating defects; a render can show a failure but can never make a check pass |
| `layout.json` | package | the declared layout and print orientations (seating rule, section 2.4) |
| self-report | package (the agent's own `clear_of` / `inside` results) | the self-report L3 only; never trusted for any other L3 |
| `requirements.md`, `part.json` files | this folder, `generalize/parts`, `enclosure/parts` | criteria, reference models, envelopes |

The judge does **not** use: the agent's code or transcript, earlier iterations' verdicts (blind per
package), `benchmarks/heldout.json`, or anything private to the owner.

### 8.2 Procedure

1. **Package gate (G0).** Check that the files in the package L2 exist and load. If the STLs are missing or unreadable, stop: DOES NOT MEET, one defect on the package L3.
2. **Load and check units.** Load each STL (8.3). Check the unit rule in the package L3; if it looks like inches or metres, record a defect, do not rescale silently.
3. **Normalise the frame** (section 1.1) by rotations of multiples of 90° and mirroring only; record the transform. All coordinates in the report are in that frame.
4. **Build the references independently** from the `part.json` files at the pose in `layout.json` (or the default layout), then every V- envelope of section 2.3. Run the sanity checks of section 2.4 and verify the seating rule.
5. **Run every applicable L3** with Python, in requirement order. Record the measured value, the limit, the margin (measured − limit, signed so negative = fail) and PASS / FAIL / UNVERIFIED / N/A. **UNVERIFIED counts as FAIL.** N/A only for physical or human checks (unless a print was made).
6. **Look at the renders** after measuring: confirm each failure is where the numbers say, and catch defects no L3 covers (a floating fragment, a feature on the wrong side). Report those as **observations** with a location; they do not change the verdict unless they make an L3 fail on re-measurement.
7. **Decide** (8.4) and **report** (8.5).

### 8.3 Measurement recipes

Use `/Users/robinonsay/python/llm-bt/venv/bin/python` with `manifold3d` (3.5.x) and `trimesh` (5.1).
trimesh's graph engine is not installed, so count shells with manifold3d's `decompose()`.

```python
import numpy as np, trimesh, manifold3d as m3d
M = m3d.Manifold

def load(path):
    tm = trimesh.load(path, force="mesh")
    man = M(m3d.Mesh(vert_properties=np.asarray(tm.vertices, np.float32),
                     tri_verts=np.asarray(tm.faces, np.uint32)))
    return tm, man            # man.status() must be NoError; len(man.decompose()) is the shell count

def clear_of(bodies, vol, c):
    pen = (bodies ^ vol).volume()           # ^ = intersection
    if pen > 0.01:
        return {"d": None, "penetration_mm3": pen, "where": (bodies ^ vol).bounding_box()}
    return {"d": bodies.min_gap(vol, c + 5.0), "penetration_mm3": 0.0}   # pass: d >= c - 0.02

def inside(vol, solids, c, big=2000):
    hull = M.hull(solids)
    out = (vol - hull).volume()
    if out > 0.01:
        return {"d": None, "outside_mm3": out, "where": (vol - hull).bounding_box()}
    shell = M.cube([big] * 3, True) - hull
    return {"d": min(vol.min_gap(solids, c + 5.0), vol.min_gap(shell, c + 5.0))}   # pass: d >= c - 0.02
```

- **Where a check fails**, save the intersection solid (or, for a near miss, the closest regions found by sampling the volume's surface and `trimesh.proximity.closest_point`) as `defect_<L3>.stl` and report its bounding box.
- **Thickness** (walls, plates, arms): cast rays along the surface normal on a 2 mm grid (or by polar angle for round parts), keep rays at least 3 mm from edges and openings, report the median and the range.
- **Hole / bore / outer diameter**: slice at mid-depth with `Manifold.slice` or `trimesh.section`; the largest inscribed circle for holes, the smallest enclosing circle (or a fitted cylinder) for bosses and outer surfaces. Depth: ray cast along the axis.
- **Planarity / flatness**: max distance of the sampled face points from their best-fit plane.
- **Printability**: rotate each body into its declared print orientation; a face with unit normal n overhangs if n_z < −cos 45° and it is not on the bed (z > 0.05). Exempt the downward faces of round holes with Ø ≤ 12 (any axis) and bridges (a downward face whose both ends rest on material at most 15 mm apart). Report offending faces grouped by patch with area and bounding box. Bed contact: area of faces with n_z < −0.999 at z < 0.05 ÷ plan bounding-box area. Thin features: local thickness < 0.8 (ray cast inward from sampled faces, excluding edges).
- **Section analysis**: slice the clip at Y = 10 %, 50 %, 90 % of its width. Fit a circle to the bore (least squares on the bore polygon's inner arc) for the bore axis, diameter and out-of-roundness. The mouth opening is the minimum distance between the two lip inner corners (the polygon vertices nearest the mouth). Wrap angle = the angle subtended at the bore axis by the bore surface (360° − the angle between the lip corners). Arm thickness, root and ℓ (A6): cast rays from the bore axis at 1° steps in θ; thickness = hit distance at the outer surface − bore radius; the root is the first θ from the lip where thickness exceeds 1.5 t; ℓ = arc length at the mid-radius.
- **Insertion (L2.3.3)**: sweep M-TUBE along −Z in 0.25 mm steps from 30 above its seat; at each step intersect with the clip; record where the intersections occur (polar angle about the bore axis) and the horizontal penetration depth per lip (the maximum, over steps, of the intersection's X extent into the lip).
- **Countersinks**: slice the flange through a screw axis; the cone angle from the section polygon's edges, the top diameter at the surface.


### 8.4 Verdict

Gates are listed in section 5.

| Class | Rule |
|---|---|
| **WRONG** | any gate fails |
| **MINOR** | no gate fails, but some weight-3 L3 fails |
| **CORRECT** | no gate fails and every weight-3 L3 passes |

**MEETS requirements** = CORRECT **and** every applicable non-physical L3 of weight 1 and 2 passes.
Anything else is **DOES NOT MEET**. Also report the weighted score Σ(weight × pass) / Σ(weight) over
applicable L3s and the worst normalised margin among weight-3 L3s (margin ÷ the criterion's
tolerance or, for one-sided limits, ÷ 0.5 mm), so a loop can see progress inside DOES NOT MEET.

Precedence rules:
- **The numbers in this document beat `part.json`.** If a `part.json` disagrees with section 2.2 or 2.4, build the references from this document, and record a defect on the requirement the stale catalogue data breaks, with where = the `part.json` field.
- **Designed contact is not interference** (section 1.3). Only penetration above 0.01 mm³ counts for the listed pairs.
- **Fixed decisions** (section 3.1) have no alternatives. Any other alternative a design uses is graded on this document's numbers and noted as an observation.
- Plug-seating or fit checks outrank checks on the size of an opening or a clearance that only exists to serve them.
- Never relax a criterion, never round in the design's favour beyond ε (section 1.2), never infer a pass from a render.

### 8.5 How to say WHERE a defect is

One entry per failed or UNVERIFIED L3 (all of them, not just the first), most severe first (gates,
then weight 3, 2, 1). Each entry must let someone who has only the package and this entry find the
problem in under a minute.

| Field | What to write |
|---|---|
| `req` | the L3 id, with its L2 and L1, e.g. `L3.2.1.1 (L2.2.1, L1.2)` |
| `status` | FAIL or UNVERIFIED |
| `body` | which printed body (or `layout`) |
| `feature` | the feature by function and, if it exists, its SCAD name |
| `face` | which surface: side, edge, outward normal |
| `location` | coordinates in mm in the normalised frame: a point, or a bounding box `[xmin, ymin, zmin]–[xmax, ymax, zmax]` |
| `measured` / `limit` / `margin` | numbers with units |
| `reference` | the reference model or envelope involved and its datum |
| `render` | the render that shows it and where in the image |
| `evidence` | saved files, e.g. `defect_L3.x.y.z.stl` |
| `needed` | the geometric change that would make the check pass, as numbers (a position, size or clearance), never a design instruction |

Group several L3s that fail for one geometric reason under the first one and list the others in
`also_fails`, but keep every L3 id in the report. Coordinates come from the measurement, not from
