# G39 2020 end drill guide for M5 tap: requirements (L1 / L2 / L3)

Held-out design of the generalisation set (`benchmarks/generalize/plan.json`, family
`fixture_adapter`). The machine-readable request (L1 only, `dev.json` format) is `design.json` next
to this file. It is modelled on E1-01 (`benchmarks/enclosure/requirements.md`), and its
catalogue parts are `extrusion_2020`, `generic_tube` (standing in for the drill bit),
`bolt_m3_iso4762` (the clamp screw) in `benchmarks/generalize/parts/`.

**Plan brief (verbatim):** "A jig that clamps onto the end of a 2020 extrusion and centres a 4.2 mm
drill on the center bore (M5 tap drill). Guide bushing hole is 4.3 mm and must be coaxial within
0.2 mm of the profile centre. The jig has a 20.2 mm square pocket 15 mm deep."

**How to read this.** L1 says what the owner needs. L2 turns each need into an engineering
requirement with numbers. L3 is a concrete check on the delivered STL. Every row has a
verification method (inspection, analysis, demonstration, test, human; as in `design.json`), a
pass/fail criterion with a number and a tolerance, and a weight (3 = critical, 2 = major, 1 =
minor); a `G` after the weight marks a gate. The "Rec" column names the measuring recipe of
section 8.4. Every number that comes from an assumption (section 3.1) is provisional until Robin
confirms it. Do not relax a criterion because an agent fails it.

## 1. Frames, tolerances, designed contact

### 1.1 Frame

The **jig frame is the catalogue frame of `extrusion_2020`** with the profile seated in the jig:
origin at the centre of the profile's end section, **Z along the profile axis, the profile
extends toward +Z from its end face**, X and Y parallel to the pocket walls. The pocket floor is the
plane Z = 0 (the profile's `END_START` plane rests on it), the pocket opens toward +Z, the pocket
rim is at Z = 15, and the guide plate lies at Z below 0 with its outer face at Z = -10 (nominal).
The drill enters the guide hole from the -Z side. A design delivered rotated by multiples of 90
degrees about Z, or flipped, is normalised into this frame by the judge and never penalised (the
profile section has 4-fold symmetry). Other frames are accepted only if `layout.json` (L2.10.1)
states the transform.

### 1.2 Tolerances

As section 8.2. "Nominal +/- t" means t plus the measurement tolerance.

### 1.3 Designed contact (never interference)

Only penetration above 0.01 mm3 counts for any pair not listed here. For the listed pairs a
distance of zero is a pass and only penetration counts.

- the profile's end face resting on the pocket floor (`END_START` on the floor plane)
- the profile touching a pocket wall, **at the extreme slack positions** of L3.3.3.1 and L3.5.3.1
- the M3 clamp screw's tip touching the profile's flat face, and its thread in its pilot hole
- the guide plate's outer face resting on a table or the print bed

The drill touching the guide hole wall is **not** designed contact: the drill must clear the hole
(L3.4.1.1) and be at most 0.10 mm smaller than it (L3.4.2.1).

## 3. Assumptions and fixed decisions

### 3.1 Assumptions (derived, with their uncertainty)

| # | Question | Value chosen | Why, and uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Actual profile section? | 20.0 x 20.0 nominal (the catalogue `width`). The pocket slack is then 0.1 per side. | Real extrusions vary by about +/- 0.1 to 0.2; a 20.2 pocket assumes the lower end. Graded on the catalogue number. |
| A2 | Profile length? | At least 100 mm; M-EXT uses 100. | Only the first 15 mm matter; the length only sets the sweep. |
| A3 | Drill model? | A plain rod Ø4.2 x 50 (`generic_tube`, solid). Flutes, point angle and shank are not modelled. | The plan lists `generic_tube` as the second part; a 4.2 mm twist drill has a body OD of 4.2. +/- 0.02. |
| A4 | Drilling depth? | The tip reaches 20 mm past the end face. | An M5 tap needs about 12 to 15 mm of full thread plus a lead; 20 gives margin. A shallower drill is a deviation, not an error. |
| A5 | Guide length? | At least 8 mm, nominal 10. | A guide length of at least 2 x the drill diameter (8.4) keeps the drill from wandering; 8.0 is the floor chosen. |
| A6 | Wall thickness? | At least 3.0 around the pocket; at least 5.0 at the clamp screw. | 3.0 is 7 to 8 perimeters of a 0.4 nozzle; 5.0 gives the M3 thread-forming screw 5 mm of engagement. +/- 1. |
| A7 | Slot geometry? | Only the catalogue channel (opening 6.2, depth 6.1) is used; the wider interior behind the 1.8 mm lip (about 11 mm) is not exploited. | Conservative: a tongue inside the channel is inside the real slot whatever the vendor. +/- 0.3 between vendors. |
| A8 | Printing? | FDM, PLA or PETG, 0.4 nozzle, 0.2 layers, bed 200 x 200 x 200. Print orientation = the pose in 1.1 (guide plate on the bed, pocket opening up). | A printed 20.2 pocket will shrink by 0.1 to 0.3 in practice; graded on the model, not on a print. Print a test and scale the pocket if it is tight. |
| A9 | Clamp screw? | M3 socket-head, ISO 4762 (`bolt_m3_iso4762`), length 10, threading into a tap-drill pilot of Ø2.5 in the wall (`tap_diameter` 2.5). Screw tip contacts the profile with 0 to 0.1 gap before tightening. The reference screw is therefore part-way out of the wall (its head stands proud of the outer face by length minus wall minus gap); its length is for the reference model only and no L3 depends on it. | The screw's `tool_access_height` 25 and `head_diameter` 5.5 come from the part.json. A thread-forming M3 in PLA works at Ø2.5 (+/- 0.1). |
| A10 | Which way is the drill fed? | From the -Z side, through the guide plate, into the profile. | Follows from a jig whose plate lies behind the pocket floor. |
| A11 | Is the slack in the profile or the jig? | Both are counted in the stack: the profile can be anywhere inside the pocket (+/- s), the guide hole is e off the pocket centre. | Worst case is the honest model of "clamp on", since one screw does not centre the profile. |

| # | Decision | Value |
|---|---|---|

| F2 | Layout of the jig | pocket and guide plate in one body: pocket Z from 0 to 15, guide plate at Z below 0 with the guide hole through it |

## 6. Feasibility

A design that meets every row exists (jig frame, mm). It was built from boxes and cylinders with
`manifold3d` and checked against M-EXT (slots and bore subtracted), M-DRILL, V-CHUCK at the five
slack poses (result noted per line).

- Outer body: square 32.2 x 32.2 (X, Y in [-16.1, 16.1]), Z from -10 to 15 (25 high).
- Pocket: 20.2 x 20.2 (X, Y in [-10.1, 10.1]) from Z = 0 to the rim at Z = 15. Floor at Z = 0.
  Walls 6.0 thick (>= 3.0). Install sweep at the five slack poses: penetration 0, gap 0 (touching
  a wall at the extreme poses, designed contact).
- Guide plate Z from -10 to 0 (guide length 10 = 2.3 x the drill diameter). Guide hole Ø4.30 on the
  axis, through to the pocket floor (e = 0). Stack = 0 + sqrt(2) x 0.1 = 0.141 <= 0.20; at every
  extreme slack pose the bore-to-hole distance is 0.141 and at the centred pose 0.
- Drill: M-DRILL Ø4.2, Z from -30 to +20: gap to the hole wall 0.05 (>= 0, <= 0.10), clear of the
  pocket. V-CHUCK (Ø10, Z from -35 to -10): empty (the plate's outer face is at Z = -10).
- Tongues: two, on the pocket's PX and NX walls: Y width 5.90 (centred), X from 7.0 to 10.1 (the
  tongue base is on the wall, tip 3.0 beyond the profile face), Z from 0 to 15. Clearance to the
  slot sides 0.15; to the slot floor 3.1; penetration 0 at the five poses.
- Clamp: one Ø2.5 hole through the PY wall (Y from 10.1 to 16.1, 6.0 thick) along -Y at X = 6.5,

## 8. Grading rules (design-specific rubric)

`benchmarks/generalize/rubric.md` did not exist when this file was written, so the generic grading
rules are repeated here, adapted from `benchmarks/enclosure/rubric.md`. If `rubric.md` exists when
you grade, read it as well: where it and this section differ, **the numbers in sections 1 to 5 of
this file win** over `rubric.md`, and both win over any `part.json` field (precedence rule 2 below).

### 8.1 Inputs and what a judge may use

A judge (a capable model, never the local model under test) gets one design package and decides
**MEETS** or **DOES NOT MEET**. It uses: the delivered STLs (every measurement), the `.scad`
source (to name features only, never to measure), the declared `layout.json`, renders (to locate
and illustrate defects; a render can show a failure but never make a check pass), the agent's own
self-report (used only for the self-report L3), this file and the catalogue `part.json` files. It
does not use the agent's transcript, earlier verdicts, or `benchmarks/heldout.json`.

### 8.2 Units, frames, tolerances

- All STLs are in **mm**. A body's largest bounding-box axis must be 5 to 300 mm; otherwise record
  a units defect and do not rescale silently. The frame is the one in section 1.1; the judge may
  rotate by multiples of 90 degrees and mirror to reach it, and records the transform.
- Measurement tolerances: length and clearance 0.02 mm, angle 0.5 degrees, interference volume
  0.01 mm3. A value passes if it meets its limit to within that tolerance. A hole's diameter is
  the largest inscribed circle of its section; a boss or pin's diameter is the smallest enclosing
  circle of its section. A band written "a to b" is inclusive.
- Numbers are measured on the delivered STLs by Python, never by eye and never from the `.scad`.

### 8.3 Precedence

1. Designed contact (section 1.3) is never interference. Only penetration above 0.01 mm3 counts,
   and only for pairs not listed there.
2. Numbers in this file beat `part.json`. If a catalogue field disagrees, build the reference from
   this file and record a defect on the requirement the stale data breaks, with `where` = the
   `part.json` field.
3. A check that cannot be run is UNVERIFIED and counts as FAIL.
4. Never relax a band, never round in the design's favour beyond the tolerance, never infer a pass
   from a render. Fixed decisions (section 3.2) admit no alternatives; an unlisted alternative is
   graded on the default numbers and noted as an observation for Robin.

### 8.4 Measuring recipes

Use `/Users/robinonsay/python/llm-bt/venv/bin/python` with `manifold3d` and `trimesh`. trimesh's
graph engine is not installed, so count shells with `manifold3d` `decompose()`, not
`trimesh.split()`.

```python
import numpy as np, trimesh, manifold3d as m3d
M = m3d.Manifold
def load(path):                                   # R-LOAD
    tm = trimesh.load(path, force="mesh")
    man = M(m3d.Mesh(vert_properties=np.asarray(tm.vertices, np.float32),
                     tri_verts=np.asarray(tm.faces, np.uint32)))
    return tm, man                                # man.status() must be NoError
shells = len(man.decompose())                     # R-SHELL: must equal the stated count
def clear_of(bodies, vol, c):                     # R-CLEAR
    pen = (bodies ^ vol).volume()                 # ^ is intersection
    if pen > 0.01: return {"d": None, "penetration_mm3": pen, "where": (bodies ^ vol).bounding_box()}
    return {"d": bodies.min_gap(vol, c + 5.0)}    # pass: pen <= 0.01 and d >= c - 0.02
def inside(vol, solids, c, big=2000):             # R-INSIDE (solids = closed assembly)
    hull = M.hull(solids); out = (vol - hull).volume()
    if out > 0.01: return {"d": None, "outside_mm3": out}
    shell = M.cube([big]*3, True) - hull
    return {"d": min(vol.min_gap(solids, c + 5.0), vol.min_gap(shell, c + 5.0))}
```

| Tag | Recipe |
|---|---|
| R-LOAD, R-SHELL | as above; manifold status and shell count per body |
| R-CLEAR, R-INSIDE | as above; save the intersection (or the two closest regions) as `defect_<L3>.stl` and report its bounding box |
| R-HOLE | slice at mid-depth (`trimesh.section`); largest inscribed circle = diameter, its centre = axis point; axis direction from two slices; depth or length by ray casting along the axis; roundness = circumscribed minus inscribed diameter |
| R-BOSS | slice at mid-height; smallest enclosing circle = OD, its centre = axis |
| R-DIST | `min_gap` between two named regions, or ray casting along a face normal on a 1 mm grid (report min, median, max); wall thickness = median of rays that start at least 3 mm from any edge or opening |
| R-SEAT | place each reference model at the pose in `layout.json`; the stated seating relation (section 2) must hold within its stated tolerance; if the declaration is missing or fails, seat the references by the rule in section 2 instead and fail the layout L3 |
| R-SWEEP | place a reference model at each pose of the stated list (rotations done with `Manifold.rotate` about the stated axis), then apply R-CLEAR at every pose; the worst pose is the reported value |
| R-APERTURE | slice a wall (or lid) at mid-thickness and take the polygon of each opening: bounding box, centroid, largest inscribed circle; unlisted openings: ray casting from outside on a 1 mm grid into the cavity, cluster the hits, confirm a 2 mm sphere passes |
| R-PRINT | rotate each body into its stated print orientation. Overhang: a face with unit normal n and n_z < -cos 45 degrees that is not on the bed and not part of a round hole of diameter 12 mm or less in a vertical wall or a bridge of 15 mm or less span (the free distance between its supports along the bridging direction; for a face enclosed on all four sides, such as a pocket's ceiling, its shorter plan dimension; the roof of a tunnel open at both ends spans the tunnel's full width). Bed contact: area of faces with n_z < -0.999 at z < 0.05 divided by the plan bounding-box area. Thin features: ray casting inward from sampled points, local thickness below 0.8 mm (excluding edges) is a defect |
| R-BBOX | `bounding_box()` per body and for the closed or assembled union |
| R-STABLE | centre of mass of the printed bodies (density 1.24 g/cm3 times the fill factor in section 3) plus the reference masses in section 3, projected onto the table, must lie inside the support polygon at the stated pose |

### 8.5 Verdict classes

Gates are the L3 rows marked `G` in section 4 (listed in section 5).

| Class | Rule |
|---|---|
| **WRONG** | any gate fails |
| **MINOR** | no gate fails, but some weight-3 L3 fails |
| **CORRECT** | no gate fails and every weight-3 L3 passes |
| **MEETS** (the zero-error state) | CORRECT and every applicable weight-1 and weight-2 L3 passes too. Physical or human L3s are N/A unless a print was made |

Also report the weighted score (sum of weight times pass, over applicable L3s, divided by the sum
of weights) and the worst normalised margin among weight-3 L3s (margin divided by the criterion's
tolerance, or by 0.5 mm for one-sided limits), so a loop can see progress inside DOES NOT MEET.

### 8.6 How to say where a defect is

One entry per failed or UNVERIFIED L3 (all of them), most severe first (gates, then weight 3, 2,
1). Group L3s that fail for one geometric reason under the first and list the rest in
`also_fails`. Fields: `req` (L3 id with its L2 and L1), `status`, `body`, `feature` (by function
and, if it exists, by its SCAD or part name), `face` (surface, side, edge, outward normal),
`location` (coordinates in mm in the section 1.1 frame, a point or a box
`[xmin, ymin, zmin]-[xmax, ymax, zmax]`), `measured` / `limit` / `margin` (signed so negative
fails), `reference` (the M- model or V- envelope and its datum), `render` (which image, where),
`evidence` (saved `defect_<L3>.stl`), `needed` (the geometric target as a number the next agent
can verify, never a design instruction such as "use a difference"). Coordinates come from the
measurement, not from the `.scad`. Render-only findings are listed separately as observations and
do not change the verdict unless re-measurement makes an L3 fail. Output `judge.md` (verdict,
class, score, worst margin, gate table, measured-values table) and `defects.json`.
